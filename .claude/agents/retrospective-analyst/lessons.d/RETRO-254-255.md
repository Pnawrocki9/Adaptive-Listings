# Retrospective-analyst lessons — RETRO-254 + RETRO-255 (2026-08-07)

**Date / entries:** 2026-08-07 · RETRO-254 (PR #685, FOLLOW-854+855+856) and RETRO-255 (PR #686,
FOLLOW-857). Per Rule AG this is a per-ticket fragment, never an append to the shared `lessons.md`
tail.

## A finding I almost missed, and why

**The settle loop's missing cardinality floor (RETRO-255 §4a LG-1, FOLLOW-865).** The brief handed
me the live sighting already framed — "the gate twice settled on a transient 5-check rollup, once
printing all checks green" — and framed it as an _observation to record against FOLLOW-848_. I very
nearly did exactly that: 848 owns the settle-loop fixtures, Rule AN says do not put two tickets on
one sentence, and folding it in would have looked like discipline.

What stopped me was asking which SIDE of the gate the defect is on. 848's ACs are all test-side
(make the fixture format able to express a changing rollup). A cardinality floor is **production
code**. Folding a P1 production gap into a P2 FROZEN test-improvement ticket would have made it
invisible — which is precisely the "prose deferrals rot" harm RETRO-253 documented at count 1, one
merge earlier, executed by me.

The general form: **Rule AN is about not splitting one sentence across two tickets. It is not about
priority. When the fold would move a P1 into a FROZEN P2, the fold is the defect.** I filed 865 with
`depends_on: FOLLOW-848` instead, which keeps the coupling without burying the priority.

Second, smaller near-miss: I nearly repeated "33 phantom FAILs" from the brief. Reproducing it in a
scratch repo gave **18 per worktree**, and the 33 only reconstructs once you measure that the corpus
was **15 files** at pre-#685 `main` and **18** at current `main` — two worktrees of different
vintage. The number in the record was right; my understanding of it would have been wrong, and the
stub's AC would have been written against a wrong mechanism.

## An axis / chain I had to trace twice

**P-35's discharge window.** The brief stated as fact that both gate PRs merging created a live
verdict-flip observation window that was not used, and asked whether the discharge is now harder or
easier. I wrote the "easier in kind, rarer in occasion" answer first — and only then went to
measure, because the brief's premise had the shape of something inferred rather than observed.

`bash scripts/check-rule-i.sh` on `main` after both merges returns **192**, the same figure
RETRO-253 recorded before them, and both PRs reported `New on this PR: 0 | fixed by this PR: 0`.
**Neither merge moved `main`'s Rule I symbol set by a single symbol, so no window existed at all.**
The question was well-posed and its premise was false, and I had already drafted an answer to it.

The lesson is narrow and I want it stated narrowly: **a brief that asserts a state of the world is
an input to verify, not a fact to reason from — and the temptation is strongest when the question
built on it is a good question.** RETRO-251 corrected its brief's premise about how three instances
were found; RETRO-253 corrected the brief's framing of a Rule S clause; this is the third
consecutive retro to find a premise error in a well-constructed brief. That is not a criticism of
the briefs — it is the predictable cost of the PM summarising a session it also executed.

Second trace-twice: RETRO-253's causal claim about `check-rule-i.sh`'s `+ 0` normalisation. #686's
R5 disproved it, and my first instinct was to record the correction as the worker's. I re-derived it
myself at `76bfd7d9` (two greps: `set -e`? `|| echo 0`?) because **a retro grading a retro's
correction should not be the third layer to repeat an untested claim** — which is the exact defect
under discussion.

## A meta-pattern in how gaps recur across agents

**This corpus grades its evidence for "what" and never for "why".** Every factual claim in RETRO-253
carries a `file:line` and most were driven. Its one _causal_ claim — "the sibling already has the
fix, copy it" — carried no evidence marker at all, propagated verbatim into FOLLOW-857's stub and
into the PM's dispatch brief, and was false. Three layers of record, one untested sentence, and the
only thing that caught it was a worker building a revert matrix at fix time.

The control is not new and I declined to mint a rule for it (RETRO-255 §6): "X is the fix because
sibling Y has X" is a **sufficiency** claim, and its test is to revert X in Y — one row of the
discrimination matrix this estate already demands, applied at claim time instead of fix time. Cost
me under a minute.

**What I am changing in my own practice:** when I write a sentence of the form "the fix is A,
because sibling B already has A", I run the two greps that would falsify it _before_ the sentence
goes in, and I mark the sentence with what I ran. My §2 blocks already separate "what I drove" from
"what I read" for facts; causal claims have been slipping through unmarked between them.

**Second meta-pattern, and it is the good news:** the two highest-value findings across these merges
came from **procedures, not incidents** — a PM-mandated post-rebase re-verification (which found a
false green live on `main` that nobody had filed) and a fix-level revert matrix (which disproved the
brief). RETRO-253 concluded that "attention follows incidents". These two merges are the first
counter-evidence in the chain, and a future retro should check whether it holds: **count how many of
the next five gate findings came from a scheduled procedure versus from something breaking.**
