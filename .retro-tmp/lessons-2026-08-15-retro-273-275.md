**A finding I almost missed, and why.** The `holdout_pct` chain looked like a textbook pass on first
read: ADR ruled, migration staged, operator step ordered, writer bound at three call sites,
red-first proven. I nearly recorded it as a clean closure on the strength of the commit body, which
is _unusually_ good — it even records that the operator's first report was checked and found false.
**That is exactly the shape that gets waved through: a verification narrative so complete that
re-running it feels redundant.** I re-ran it anyway (`system.columns` against prod ClickHouse) and
the claim held — but the same probe returned `max(ts) = 2026-06-29` and `count() = 19`, which is the
finding I would have missed entirely: the column is correct, live, and **capturing nothing**,
because the table has not taken a row in 47 days. A closure verified on the axis it names can still
be meaningless on an axis nobody named. **The lesson is not "distrust the commit body" — it is "the
probe that confirms a claim usually returns three other numbers, and those are free."**

**An axis/chain I had to trace twice.** `getWaitUntil`. First pass I accepted #747's framing — "the
post-ACK ClickHouse write was never registered" — because it is true, well-evidenced, and the
nightly E2E went green on it. Second pass I asked the question the framing suppressed: _how many
call sites does this function have?_ Three, not one, since PR #429. The fix covers all three (it is
at the shared helper), so there was no bug to find — but the **analysis** covered one, the
**audibility fix** covered one, and the retracted assumption ("the promise is still dispatched")
survives verbatim on the other two legs of the same function. One of those legs is the chat→Modal
dispatch whose production traffic axis has been open as FOLLOW-892 for weeks. **I got there by
grepping the symbol rather than reading the diff — the diff shows what changed, and the defect was
in what didn't.** When a PR fixes a shared helper, the number of consumers is a mandatory question,
and the PR body will not answer it, because the author was debugging one of them.

**A meta-pattern in how gaps recur across agents.** Three of this batch's findings are the same
shape at three scales, and each was produced by a _different_ agent doing careful work:

- `#742` swept three files and claimed the repo (backend-engineer);
- `#743` caught that, swept the repo on one key, and missed the same falsehood spelled another way
  (backend-engineer, correcting itself);
- `#741` derived a detector vocabulary from an exhaustive 198-line corpus enumeration — and a claim
  outside it was written 15 hours later, by a different agent, in a different app (architect).

**The unifying failure is not carelessness; it is that every one of these controls was calibrated
against a snapshot of a corpus that keeps moving.** The estate's instinct is to make the sweep more
exhaustive. That does not help: `#741`'s enumeration _was_ exhaustive, on the day. What helps is
making the calibration re-derivable or expiring — which is what P-52 is about, and why I incremented
it rather than promoting it (one prior is not two, and I wanted to promote it).

**On my own restraint.** Zero promotions across three entries and sixteen PRs. P-52 sits at count 2
with one prior (RETRO-271); P-55 minted at count 1. RETRO-272 held P-53 at exactly this arithmetic
two days ago, and applying the same standard when the candidate is _mine_ is the only version of the
threshold that means anything.

**Process note for the next batch pass.** RETRO-272's lesson recommended a standing "what is
`main`'s current check state" read for accumulated batches. I did the equivalent and it paid twice:
reading `gh run list --workflow=e2e-smoke.yml` gave me the **scheduled** green that the PRs' own CI
could not prove, and `gh secret list` settled the Slack half-wire in one call. **Both answers were
about the world, not the repo, and neither PR body contained them.** For a batch this size the cheap
standing set is: the workflow's own run history filtered to the `schedule` trigger, the secret list,
and one live probe per prod-state claim the batch makes. Three calls, and they decided three
findings.
