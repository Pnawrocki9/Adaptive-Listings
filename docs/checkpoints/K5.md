## The N3 record, including what failed

On the same build, run 1 was RED and runs 2 to 4 GREEN. Earlier harness versions also went RED; the
failures fell into three kinds:

- **No consent banner (run 1).** `/consent-text.json` took 8175 ms against the SDK's 3000 ms budget.
  The SDK then shows no banner and sends nothing, as ADR-0021 §D4 requires. Other runs took 250 to
  1234 ms. That the budget caused it is inferred from the duration and the missing banner; the abort
  itself was not observed. **It is a product behaviour, not fixed:** FOLLOW-1461.
- **The host re-renders or reloads around its silent SSO answer.** A click in that moment can be
  lost, and the seller's copy comes back on the button until the next decision lands
  (`adapt.skipped` `stale`). N3 now waits for the SSO answer and the adapted button, and clicks
  again up to 3 times, counting the clicks. A button that never opens the prompt stays RED. The
  cause is inferred from timing. **Not fixed in the host:** FOLLOW-1462.
- **The harness itself:** a late backend log line, a slow host banner, and environment errors of the
  PM's scripts (stale stacks, secrets not exported, a stack that did not start). Those runs say
  nothing about the product.

Load average was 4 to 16 during these runs, on a machine with 4.9 GB of RAM.

## The batch, and what it does not show

`--arms 40`, run-scoped: adapted 20 of 40 sessions converted, holdout 20 of 40. **These are equal by
construction:** the conversion plan is arm-blind and scripted. All conversions were signed.

The reader read `relative_lift_pct` -77.68, `confidence` 95 %, over 1340 adapted and 355 holdout
sessions: `poolIsRun` is false, because the 7-day window holds earlier scripted sessions from this
work. **That number is not the run's and says nothing about the product.** The local tables were not
cleared; the run-scoped counts are the figures of record, as the runbook allows.

Localhost proves that the pipeline counts the right events by the right formula. It does not show
that adaptation sells. That reading exists only in production, after at least 30 real sessions per
arm.
