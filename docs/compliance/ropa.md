### Joint Controller Analysis

For the avoidance of doubt, this section addresses three distinct controllership classifications:

**(a) Behavioral data captured on tenant websites — Tenant is Controller, Time2Show is Processor.**

Time2Show and its tenants (real estate agencies) are not joint controllers for behavioral data
collected on tenant websites. The tenant determines the purposes of the listing website (real estate
marketing) and engages Time2Show to perform personalisation processing pursuant to that purpose.
Time2Show determines the technical means of the personalisation processing but only in service of
the tenant's purpose. This is a Controller → Processor relationship within the meaning of GDPR art.
4(8) and art. 28, as clarified by EDPB Guidelines 07/2020 on the concepts of controller and
processor (Version 2.0, adopted 7 July 2021), paragraphs 23–28.

**(b) Operational data — Time2Show is Sole Controller.**

For Time2Show's own operational data (audit logs of staff actions, tenant account management,
billing, regulatory compliance records, global archetype model training output), Time2Show is sole
controller. Purposes and means are determined by Time2Show without tenant input. This includes:
`staff_audit_log` (Activity 10), `tenants`, `users`, `api_keys` (Activity 6), billing records and
Stripe customer/subscription data (Activity 11), A/B bandit weights (Activity 12, no personal data),
and global archetype embeddings (Activity 9, post-DP-aggregation, no personal data).

**(c) Engagement Score feature — Time2Show is Sole Controller.**

The Engagement Score is a derived metric computed by Time2Show that quantifies the engagement
intensity of a visitor session based on behavioral signals (dwell time, scroll depth, interaction
count, etc.). The Engagement Score is exposed back to tenants as part of the tenant analytics
dashboard.

**Classification rationale:**

- **Purposes:** Time2Show alone determines what the Engagement Score measures and what it is used
  for (product analytics, model improvement, tenant-visible engagement reporting). Tenants have no
  input into the design, weighting, or interpretation of the score.
- **Means:** Time2Show alone determines the algorithmic logic, the input features, the normalisation
  approach, and the storage architecture.
- **Tenant role:** Tenants are recipients of the Engagement Score output, not joint determiners of
  purposes or means. Tenants display the score to their staff but do not influence its computation.

**Why this is not Joint Controllership (art. 26 GDPR):** Joint controllership under art. 26 requires
"jointly determine the purposes and means" — the CJEU in Wirtschaftsakademie (Case C-210/16),
Jehovan todistajat (Case C-25/17), and Fashion ID (Case C-40/17) clarified that joint controllership
arises where each party makes a meaningful, substantive determination of purposes or essential
means. A tenant receiving a pre-computed score as part of a SaaS dashboard does not meaningfully
determine purposes or essential means of the score's computation. This is therefore Sole
Controllership by Time2Show, with tenants as recipients under the SaaS contract.

As Sole Controller for the Engagement Score, Time2Show bears full GDPR controller obligations for
that processing: lawful basis identification (LI under art. 6(1)(f) with documented LIA),
transparency to data subjects (via tenant privacy notices that reference the score), and data
subject rights fulfilment.

### Sentry redaction — what actually runs, per app (FOLLOW-739)

This row previously described a "`beforeSend` hook with PII patterns regex" verified by a "CI check
on scrubber config". **No PII-pattern matching exists anywhere in this estate, and redaction is not
applied uniformly.** Corrected 2026-08-04 against the code, and stated per app because four
applications emit to Sentry under one sub-processor relationship:

**1. Python tier — `apps/intent-engine` and its two Rule-J mirrors (`apps/llm-gateway`,
`apps/data-quality`).** A `before_send` hook, `_scrub_chat_intent_exception_value`
(`apps/intent-engine/src/observability.py`), replaces the exception `value` string with a fixed
placeholder — **only** on events tagged `area=chat_intent`, and **only** that one field. It is a
blanket, tag-gated overwrite, not a scan for PII shapes. Events tagged `onboarding` or
`schema_validation` pass through unmodified (those paths carry no buyer chat text). Breadcrumbs,
`extra`, `request` and `contexts` are **not** touched on any path.

Why it exists: the chat-intent extraction path can raise a `JSONDecodeError`/`ValueError` whose
`str(exc)` embeds a fragment of the model's raw JSON response, which can itself echo buyer chat text
— the one field that could carry message content into Sentry.

Verification artifacts, named rather than paraphrased:

- `apps/intent-engine/src/test_observability.py` — pytest, runs in the normal CI test matrix; pins
  the hook's behaviour, including a buyer-text sentinel that must not survive redaction.
- `scripts/check-sentry-init-singleton.sh` — wired into `ci.yml`. This is a **bypass guard** ("is
  every `sentry_sdk.init(` routed through the shared hardened helper"), NOT an inspection of the
  hook's contents. It cannot detect a weakened `before_send`; the pytest above is what does.

**2. `apps/control-plane` (TypeScript / Next.js) — NO redaction of any kind.**
`sentry.server.config.ts`, `sentry.client.config.ts` and `sentry.edge.config.ts` each call
`Sentry.init()` with no `beforeSend` and no scrubbing. Since FOLLOW-811 (2026-08-05) all three set
`sendDefaultPii: false` explicitly; that is the SDK's existing default made non-implicit, not a new
control, and it changes nothing about what these events carry.

**Residual-risk assessment (accepted, with a stated boundary).** Raw buyer chat text does not enter
this application, so there is today no message content for a scrubber to remove. Verified by grep
over `apps/control-plane/src`, not assumed:

- Chat intent reaches the control plane already **derived**: `lib/chat-intent-cache.ts` reads the
  dimension vector written by Modal from Redis. Raw `messages` is not a field on the payload model
  (see `C-07-chat-retention-scope.md`).
- The LLM prompt is built from archetype, playbook copy, behavioural **event labels** and
  agency-curated listing metadata (`LlmGatewayInput`, `lib/llm-gateway.ts`) — no buyer-authored free
  text.
- Across all `Sentry.captureException` / `captureMessage` call sites in the app, the `extra` and
  `contexts` payloads carry identifiers and status values only: `tenant_id`, `listing_id`,
  `archetype`, `session_id`, HTTP status, table name, window/cutoff. The single site that captures
  generated text is the directive fact-check violation in `lib/llm-gateway.ts`, which embeds
  LLM-generated listing copy grounded on agency-curated metadata — not buyer-supplied content.

**The boundary this accepts:** control-plane is unscrubbed _by construction_, not _by control_.
Nothing mechanically prevents a future change from putting buyer text into a captured exception or
an `extra` payload. Whether that warrants its own hook was decided under **FOLLOW-811**
(2026-08-05): **no hook**, residual risk formally accepted with six named re-review triggers,
recorded in `dpia.md` §2.7.1. Do not re-argue it here — amend §2.7.1.

**One correction FOLLOW-811 established, which widens this row's scope:** in `apps/control-plane`
the application-log sink and the Sentry sink are **not independent**. `consoleIntegration()` is a
default integration of the installed Node SDK (`@sentry/node-core@10.50.0`,
`build/cjs/sdk/index.js:41`), so every `console.error`/`console.warn` in that app (115 and 33
non-test call sites) is also a Sentry input, arriving as a `category: "console"` breadcrumb on the
next captured event — and breadcrumbs are among the fields stated above as never modified on any
path. Proven by `apps/control-plane/src/lib/__tests__/sentry-capture-path.test.ts`, which drives a
real capture and asserts a buyer-text sentinel written to `console.error` survives into the outgoing
envelope. Practical consequence for this record: **content logged by that app is disclosed to two
sub-processors (Vercel and Sentry), not one.** Vercel runtime-log retention is **not recorded
anywhere in this repository** and is deliberately not guessed; what is established is that no log
drain is configured (`apps/control-plane/vercel.json` declares none; no drain reference exists in
`infra/`), so nothing copies those logs into a Time2Show-controlled long-lived store. Establishing
the actual Vercel retention figure for the Vercel row above remains an open gap.

**FOLLOW-838 correction (2026-08-05) — the paragraph above is app-scoped and was read as if the
other apps were exempt; they are not.** The console↔Sentry coupling is a property of the SDK default
integration lists, not of `apps/control-plane`. It holds identically in the Cloudflare SDK:
`@sentry/cloudflare@10.50.0` `build/cjs/sdk.js:29` is `core.consoleIntegration()`, the last entry of
`getDefaultIntegrations()`, which `init()` applies whenever the caller passes no `integrations` key
(`sdk.js:36-38`) — and `apps/ingest/src/observability.ts:71-82` passes none. Three consequences for
this record, each read from the installed code:

- **`apps/ingest` discloses its logs to two sub-processors as well** (Cloudflare and Sentry),
  whenever `SENTRY_DSN_INGEST` is set. It is unset in production today, so the Sentry half is inert
  **by environment, not by control** (`observability.ts:72-75`); the operator action that sets it

  `apps/ingest/src/handlers/chat-nlp-sentry-capture-path.test.ts`, whose positive-control block

- **The exposed surface in `apps/ingest` is larger than a `console.error` grep suggests.** There are
  10 non-test `console.error` call sites and 0 `console.warn`/`log`/`info`, but a further 25
  non-test `logger.*` calls, and `@estalara/shared`'s Worker-safe logger emits through `console.log`
  (`packages/shared/src/observability/logger.ts:111`). Every structured log line in that app is
  therefore also a Sentry breadcrumb input.
- **The Python tier is NOT affected on this axis** — stated explicitly so it is not inferred from

What `apps/ingest` does about it is a call-site redaction rather than a hook, decided under
FOLLOW-838 and recorded with its evidence and re-review triggers in `dpia.md` §2.7.2. Two open items
noted there rather than fixed: `sendDefaultPii` is not pinned explicitly in

### Modal application (stdout) logs — destination, retention, access, and the FOLLOW-812 correction

The Modal row above previously described only "session embedding computation inputs; archetype
aggregation inputs." That omitted a second, narrower data flow: `apps/intent-engine/src/nlp.py`'s
chat-intent extraction primary-failure branch writes a diagnostic line to stdout (Modal's own
application log), and — before this correction — that line embedded the swallowed exception's full
message (`str(exc)`), which for a `_parse_response` `JSONDecodeError`/`ValueError` can echo a

## Appendix C — Material Change Triggers for ROPA/DPIA Re-Assessment

The following events constitute "material change" requiring ROPA and/or DPIA re-assessment within 30
days:

1. **New sub-processor engagement** or **change of sub-processor jurisdiction**
2. **New category of personal data** processed (e.g., addition of authenticated user features)
3. **New purpose of processing** not covered by existing lawful basis assessment
4. **Change of data residency** (e.g., adding a new region)
5. **Loss of DPF certification** by any DPF-certified sub-processor
6. **New supervisory authority guidance** materially affecting current processing (e.g., EDPB
   opinion, ICO determination on fingerprinting, UODO enforcement action)
7. **Regulatory enforcement action** against a similarly-situated controller in the EU/UK/UAE
8. **Material change in DPF status** (suspension, invalidation, withdrawal)
9. **Change in establishment status** (e.g., CEO relocation outside Poland, EU subsidiary formation,
   US-resident senior staff additions)
10. **Joint Controller analysis re-trigger** for any new feature that surfaces controller-determined
    outputs to tenants
